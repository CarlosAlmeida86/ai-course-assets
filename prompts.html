Lesson 1 — From Inbox to a Reply in Your Own Tone

Entry note (say or type this first if you are pasting)

These are the five emails I want you to work with. I will paste them one at a time.

Triage

Muse, look at these five emails and tell me: what needs my action today, what can wait, and what is just information? Give it back in three short lists, in order of urgency, with one line on why each one is there — and use each message's date.

Draft

Now draft the reply to Marina. She wants to move from Thursday at three. Offer Friday at ten or Monday at four — the two slots I just confirmed. Don't send anything — just show me the draft.

Tone correction

Same offer, but in my tone: warm and direct, three sentences at most, and end by asking which one she prefers.

Second-AI review (in ChatGPT)

ChatGPT, review this reply against the client's original message. Check three things only: the tone — warm and direct; any missing detail the client asked about; and whether the reply is ready to send. Answer briefly.

Lesson 2 — Create Your AI Assistant

Setup + read-back

From now on, you are Nova, my personal assistant for everyday organization. Your personality with me is friendly, upbeat and concise. Follow these rules: one — when you draft writing for me, be warm and direct, use at most three sentences, and end with the next step. Two — never send or book anything; show me first. Three — when a message is for a client or anyone outside, switch to a standard professional voice: polite and clear, no jokes, and my personal style stays out of it. Confirm back to me the name, role, personality and rules you saved.

The personal test

Nova, I'm done for today. What's something fun on my list for Saturday? And draft a relaxed message to my friend Leo inviting him over for a barbecue on Saturday afternoon.

The client draft

A client, Paula, just wrote asking to move her Friday 2 p.m. appointment to next week. Draft her a reply offering Tuesday at 10 a.m. or Wednesday at 3 p.m. Draft only — don't send.

The boundary correction (tone)

That draft broke the boundary in my profile: it used my personal tone with a client. Rewrite it in the standard professional voice from my profile — polite, clear, no jokes — and change nothing else.

The fact-check correction (facts)

Now check your draft against my instruction: I offered TWO times — Tuesday at 10 a.m. and Wednesday at 3 p.m. Your draft dropped Wednesday. Restore it, and remove any promise to rearrange the schedule.

The fresh-task test

Nova, new task: draft a short message to the building manager asking when the elevator maintenance ends.

The due-date control

Research — hypotheses first, labeled as hypotheses

Label every group a hypothesis. Do not claim any of them will buy.

The evidence table

Muse, turn these findings into an evidence table: hypothesis, where I checked, what I actually found, and a grade — interest signal, weak, or partial. Keep anything I did not personally find out of the table.

Build the offer (price blank for now)

Muse, build a one-page offer from the skill card for people with a confirmed move date. Give it a clear name. State: who it is for, what the customer gets, exactly what is included, exactly what is NOT included, how long it takes, and what the customer must provide. Leave the price blank for now. Do not use the words 'guaranteed', 'passive income', or any earnings claim.

Cost it and test a price

Muse, using these costs, show me both test prices with every step of the arithmetic visible: the listed direct costs, the amount remaining before my time, and the amount remaining per hour after the listed direct costs at each test price, before taxes and other expenses. List anything the estimate leaves out. Do not project monthly or yearly income.

Use the findings I gave you; do not treat listings or requests as proof that anyone paid. Do not rewrite the offer yet, and do not tell me it will sell.
